     Abstract

      This study examines whether the rise in the self-containment of Seoul's  official living zones between 2020 and 2025
  indicates that the mismatch between these zones and actual travel has been resolved. Using origin–destination data from
  Seoul Living Movement  statistics at the administrative-dong  level, the  official zones were compared with two references
  constructed within each autonomous district: random comparison boundaries drawn only from spatial adjacency, and virtual
  boundaries derived from the same travel data by consensus community detection. The internal-flow ratio rose in every
   district, but random boundaries showed a rise of similar size, and the rise came from a higher share of trips staying within
  the zone in every trip type rather than from a change in trip composition, and  it was larger at weekends. Meanwhile, the
  share of trips judged differently by the official and virtual boundaries showed no significant decline, and the self-containment
  gap between them widened, about half of  it because of travel change itself. Against size-matched random boundaries the
   official zones showed generally higher  internal-flow  ratios; a small  set of boundary dongs was repeatedly  identified as
  candidates for review in both years, and a separate sequential reassignment of a few boundary dongs brought the official
  zones close to the  virtual boundaries. Rising self-containment should therefore not be read as resolved mismatch, and
  living-zone review can focus on the boundary dongs identified.
